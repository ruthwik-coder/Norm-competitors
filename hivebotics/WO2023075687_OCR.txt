Robot Alignment And Manipulation
Technical Field
The present invention relates, in general terms, to systems and methods for
aligning an autonomous cleaning robot with an object, manipulating a robotic
arm of that robot, and interchanging tool heads of an end effector.
Background
While the traditional manual method is effective for toilet cleaning, it is labour
intensive and time consuming. Moreover, its cleaning effectiveness decreases
as the cleaning fluid becomes contaminated. A need exists in the art for an
improved method for cleaning a Surface to provide an affordable cleaning device
for automating wet floor cleaning in the home.
In many large buildings, such as hospitals, large retail stores, cafeterias, and
the like, there is a need to clean on a daily or nightly basis, and attempts have
been made to address this problem by the development of industrial
floor cleaning "robots".
It has become increasingly advantageous to automate certain routine
processes, particularly those that are manual labour intensive. Processes
involving manual labour can often be difficult to automate on the basis that they
require high consistency and low tolerance for error.
It would be desirable to overcome or ameliorate at least one of the above-
described problems, or at least to provide a useful alternative.

guided robot alignment. The robotic arm automatically adjusts to best fit
intended trajectory along a surface in a real environment. This eliminates need
to pre-train the robot and calibrate it for fixed use case, hence increasing its
versatility.
Advantageously, some embodiments use real-time surface normal estimation —
e.g. those that use point cloud, where a surface normal for each point in the
cloud is used in surface normal estimation. This enables the robot arm to
maintain constant consistent force and distance when manipulating along a
surface. For example, a distance from the surface can be calculated and the
robot arm thereby controlled (i.e. manipulated) to maintain a consistent
distance from the cleaning surface.
Advantageously, some embodiments provide an autonomous tool switching
mechanism. These embodiments are able to switch different tools on robot
without human intervention.

Detailed description
The present disclosure relates to autonomous robots with robotic arms. The
robots and arms disclosed herein can reduce human involvement in repeated
menial tasks (i.e., cleaning toilet bowl, mirrors and welding) and automate tasks
requiring multiple tools and surface contact (such as cleaning, welding,
manufacturing). In general, the proposed robots and arms can occupy an
important position in people's lives because they can automatically do repetitive
tasks such as cleaning objects with limited need of manual participation.
In order for the proposed robots (such as cleaning robots) to execute pre-
defined interactions with scene objects (for example as shown in Figure 1, the
robotic arm 102 manipulates along surface of toilet bowl 106 to perform
cleaning task), it is essential that the alignment of the robot to the scene object
is similar to the alignment when the trajectory path is trained. As such, a system
to determine robot alignment needs to be developed. In the present disclosure,
a method for autonomous computer vision guided robot arm trajectory
alignment is proposed. Said method allows the robotic arm to align to target
cleaning feature (such as toilet bowl, urinal, sink and mirror). In some

As will be discussed in detail, the proposed method involves recognition of the
target feature, followed by determination of angle and distance from the

the toilet to limit the classification model to only relevant features.

At step 202, a suitable target object in the scene can be detected (such as toilet
bowl 302, potted plant 304 and cup 306) using bounding box classification per

trained on the COCO dataset, and includes simple functionality for test time
augmentation and hyper parameter evolution. It is an object detection model
that combines bounding box prediction and object classification into a single end
to end differentiable network.

Step 1202: determining a distance between the autonomous cleaning

Step 1204: adjusting a position of the autonomous cleaning robot if the

In particular, once the cleaning robot is aligned to be normal to the feature as
well as horizontally aligned to the centre of the feature, the pre-set expected
distance range from the feature origin is compared against the distance
obtained. This pre-set distance is calculated for the different features in order
to allow maximum freedom for the arm motion and to reduce chances of
collision. If the current distance is greater than expected range, the robot is
deemed to be too far away from feature. On the contrary, if the current distance
is less than the expected range, the robot is deemed too near to the feature.

The present disclosure now discloses details regarding the method of generating
path trajectory. First, a suitable shape based on the edge is used to model the
cleaning surface of the toilet feature. (i.e., ellipse 1409 for inner toilet bowl).
Second, the Cartesian points of all the point clouds points located within the
shape is stored in an array of tuples of the form (cartesian_coordinate,
pass count), where pass_count is initialised to 0. The centre of the shape
detected in step 1404 is identified as the origin located at Cartesian point (xo,
yo, zo). Third, forming an array of waypoints comprises sampling the point cloud
at the cleaning surface to produce a point cloud for each waypoint; and storing
the point cloud for each waypoint in memory. Said point cloud may be generated
from a depth image captured by a camera system. In particular, point clouds
lying along the shape where zi = zo are sampled ((xi,yi,zi), pass_count) stored
in an array denoting the waypoint for the arm cleaning trajectory. Said array
can be formalized as:
waypoints = [((x1,y1,z0), 0), ((x2,y2,z0), 0), ...].

In some embodiments, the autonomous robot is aligned as follows. First, a
suitable target object in the scene is detected (such as a toilet bowl) using
bounding box classification. Second, fixed feature points 3302 on the target
image are identified (such as rear edges of toilet bowl) per Figure 33. In
particular, A vector 3304 is drawn to the feature 3302 and the displacement
and angle of the robotic arm with respect to the feature point 3302 is detected.
Third, the current angle and displacement is compared to the pre-defined target
angle and displacement and the necessary adjustment to the robot's pose is
suggested. When the robot is within an acceptable threshold for angle and
displacement, it is determined to be aligned to the object.
In fact, the present disclosure proposes a real time surface normal estimation
mechanism. Real time surface normal estimation allows the robot to detect the
normal to the surface as it is manipulating and correct its trajectory in order to
maintain a constant displacement from the surface. In particular, depth camera
is used to generate a visual 3D map of the environment. Point cloud data points
are analysed and surface is identified. Using surface normal estimation, the
normal to the surface is identified (shown in Figure 33 as arrows 3304). As the
robot arm moves along its predefined trajectory, the normal displacement of
the arm from the surface is computed and the value is then processed. If the

displacement (x cm) is different from the expected displacement at the points
3302, then the arm adjusts its joint angles to move the arm closer or further
away from the surface based on the requirement. It will be appreciated that in
this way the robot arm is able to maintain a constant distance with the surface
and such design also improves the reliability of the system as issues with
alignment of the robot with target feature can be compensated for through the
surface normal estimation correction.

The present disclosure also relates to an autonomous tool switching mechanism
illustrated in Figure 35. The purpose of said switching mechanism is that the
autonomous robot needs to be able to wield different tools automatically in order
to perform multiple tasks through the same platform. This is achieved by
designing and implementing the following sub-systems. The first sub-system is
a tool holder that is able to allow passive swapping of its end effector (see 3400
of Figure 34). The second sub-system is a disposable tool dispersal system. The
third sub-system is an active tool swapping mechanism that allows robotic arm
to switch between different tool holders. One pneumatic plate is the end effector
of the robotic arm while the other plate is attached to the top of the tool holder.
In order to support hot swapping of different tool holders, a pneumatic system
is used at the end effector of the robotic arm. It consists of two base plates (see
the third sub-system mentioned above), one of which is attached to the end of
the robotic arm and the other is on individual tool holders. When the arm
attaches to a tool holder, the pneumatic plates will align and join each other.
The tool-holder in turn has been designed such that it can connect and
disconnect with various disposable tools. For example, the tool holder (see the
first sub-system above), is able to load a disposable toilet brush from the toilet
brush dispenser by aligning and then pushing down onto the brush. A fork finger
3402 on the tool holder 3400 locks on to the inner wali of the disposable toilet
brush.
When a tool (such as the toilet brush) is dispensed from the dispenser (see the
second sub-system above), the dispenser will activate its linear actuator which
will push up the next toilet brush to the top ready to be dispensed next. When
a disposable tool like the toilet brush is to be disposed of, the tool holder has a
latch which releases the fork finger lock when pressed hence releasing the tool
attached. Such design is used for disposal of used tools after they are no longer
required.
